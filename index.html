How this works — your file is never uploaded anywhere; all processing happens on your device. Images (JPEG/PNG/WebP) are re-encoded by searching JPEG quality and, if needed, scaling dimensions down. Video and audio are re-encoded with a real copy of FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, running entirely in the browser tab — video is muxed to H.264/AAC MP4, audio to MP3, at a bitrate calculated from your target size and the clip's duration. One exception to "nothing over the network": the first time you compress a video or audio file, your browser fetches the FFmpeg engine itself (~30MB, from jsDelivr's CDN) — a one-time download of the *tool*, never your file — and it's cached after that. Everything else is compressed with the browser's built-in gzip, which is lossless and best-effort, so it can't be forced to hit an exact size, especially for formats with little redundancy left (zip, jpg, mp3…).
